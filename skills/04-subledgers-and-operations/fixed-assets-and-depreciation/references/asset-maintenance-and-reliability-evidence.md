# Asset Maintenance and Reliability Evidence

This reference is a self-contained synthesis prepared from Raghuvir Pai,
Gopinath Chattopadhyay, and Anne Gibbs (eds.), *Advances in Intelligent Asset
Management and Maintenance* (2026). It adapts maintenance engineering evidence
to accounting review; it does not determine an accounting policy or replace
current IFRS, tax, engineering, or safety review.

## Asset-life evidence

Maintain a link between the asset register, component hierarchy, maintenance
history, condition indicators, failure events, work orders, costs, downtime,
availability, safety exposure, and decisions to repair, upgrade, replace, or
retire. Separate commercial, technical, technological, and legal life; they are
different decision variables.

## Life-extension review

When failure data is sparse, state the gap and use documented maintenance text,
expert evidence, condition data, risk, cost, reliability, maintainability, and
availability to support a qualified decision. Compare life-cycle cost, risk, and
performance for intervention options. Link approved capitalisation,
componentisation, impairment indicators, useful-life changes, and disposals to
the evidence and reviewer decision.

## Control checks

- Maintenance cost is not automatically capital expenditure.
- A predictive model or condition score is an indicator, not an accounting conclusion.
- Register-to-GL tie-out includes additions, components, depreciation, impairment, disposals, and approved changes.
- Missing history remains an uncertainty and is not replaced with invented failure rates.
